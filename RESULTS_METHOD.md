# The binary debiaser — method results

**Status: draft results for a method-first paper.** Companion to `design.md`,
`experiments_v2–v6.md`, `SUMMARY_V5.md`. Every number here is computed from a
measured artifact file (no hardcoded baselines, R2-bug rule); scripts named per
section. All CIs are percentile bootstrap, 10k resamples, RNG seed 0.

**Why the reframe.** This program was built to test a family blind-spot law, with
the sign-only edit as its instrument. The law's own primary estimand does not
survive (§7). The instrument does — it is the best-validated object in the
project, and it had never been written up on its own terms because it was never
the headline.

---

## 0. One-paragraph result

A sign-only (binary) LoRA delta, distilled from a designer model's **endogenous**
elicitation and applied by negation, removes social bias under a strict
collateral budget (ΔMMLU ≥ −0.02, ppl ratio ≤ 1.10). The effect is carried by the
**sign structure**: randomising the signs at identical scale and sparsity drops
removal from +0.206 to +0.008 (paired Δ = **+0.198 [+0.175, +0.222]**, n = 502).
It survives both pre-registered nulls with a gap of +0.55 to +0.63. Binarisation
costs little — **86.6 % of full-precision** task-vector negation at ≤3.8B, and
**125.6 %** (no cost) at 7–9B. Scale granularity is irrelevant, and the edit
sparsifies aggressively: **99 % of entries can be zeroed with no measurable
loss**, giving a **0.4–1.6 MB** patch.

**On removal, the edit does not lead alone.** Against two inference-time methods
built from the *same* elicited corpora — activation steering and SentenceDebias
projection — at matched collateral and matched selection space, **all three are
statistically tied** (edit − steering = **−0.010 [−0.085, +0.056]**; edit −
SentenceDebias = **−0.028 [−0.108, +0.046]**; n = 8 cells; §11). All three clearly
beat the standard trained baseline, DPO (edit − DPO = **+0.293 [+0.037, +0.535]**),
and prompt-level self-debiasing fails the budget outright in 16 of 18
conditions (§6). Removal on this signal is not scarce; the artifact is. The contribution is therefore **not** "the
edit removes more bias" but what a weight edit uniquely offers at equal removal:
a **persistent, mergeable, sub-megabyte, inference-cost-free** artifact, together
with the deployment-honest budget protocol, the sign-structure result, and
`contrast_gap` as an endogenous rule for choosing the edit source before paying
for training. This framing was **pre-registered before any steering number
existed** (PREREGISTRATION.md, v7) as the branch to take if the comparison tied.
Generality: the method clears both nulls on **5 axes** across three benchmark
families, and removal tracks available bias with **corr(pre_skew, removal) =
+0.910** (§7). The recommended sign source is **`sign(Δ_fp)`** — STE training ties
it and is retired (§11b).

## 6. External baseline — prompt-level self-debiasing (Schick)

The reviewer question for any weight-editing method is "why not just prompt?".
The edit is always-on, so a deployed instruction is too: the prompt is prepended
to the bias probes **and** the MMLU items **and** the perplexity corpus, then
scored with the identical `bias_reduction` / `collateral_ok` machinery. Three
prompt strengths, so a null reads as "prompting fails" not "that wording failed".

**16 of 18 prompt conditions fail the collateral budget.** Best in-budget removal
per cell, against the edit on the same cell:

| cell | best in-budget prompt | edit (self → cross) |
|---|---|---|
| phi\|crows | **+0.067** | +0.316 → +0.632 |
| phi\|occ | −0.013 | +0.316 → +0.632 |
| qwen\|occ | *none in budget* | +0.378 → +0.582 |
| qwen\|crows | *none in budget* | −0.047 → +0.101 |
| gemma\|occ | *none in budget* | +0.674 → +0.842 |
| gemma\|crows | *none in budget* | +0.123 |

Two things worth reporting beyond the headline:

- **Prompting's gains are bought with capability.** The strong prompt does remove
  bias (qwen\|crows +0.176, gemma\|crows +0.122) — at ppl ratio 1.28 and 1.11.
  That is degradation, not debiasing, and only the budget exposes it.
- **Prompting can make bias worse.** `mild` raises occ_gender skew on qwen
  (0.326 → 0.407, removal **−0.081**), and also on phi and gemma. A vague
  fairness instruction *increases* occupational gender skew.

Source: `run_prompt_baseline.py` → `results/v6trace/prompt_baseline.json`.

## 8. Reproducibility

The pipeline is **bit-deterministic**: the v5 x2 bridge panel and an independent
v6 rerun on patched code agree on **108/108 sweeps, max difference 0.000000**.
Per-cell values carry no run-to-run noise; all variation is across the 3 seeds.

Two engineering results worth stating because they gate replication:

- `train_task_vector` materialises the dense fp32 weight delta (~8.6 GB for an
  attn set on a 7–9B target). A cell held four simultaneously (dwb + dwd +
  contrast + binarize output ≈ 34 GB), which OOM-killed a 32 GB host 9 times.
  In-place contrast + hand-over binarisation cut the peak to 17.6 GB, verified
  **bit-exact** (max |ΔE| = 0.000e+00).
- Every α of the collateral search is now persisted (`alpha_trace.jsonl`), so
  penalty-vs-budget and collateral-per-unit-removal curves are recoverable
  without re-running. Previously only the scalar best-in-budget survived.

## 9. What the method paper does NOT yet have

- **Trained baselines are partial.** DPO, SentenceDebias, INLP and the STE arm are
  run (§11, §11b). **PCGU** (repo pins `torch==1.4.0`/`transformers==4.10.3`) and
  **FairLoRA** (no canonical public implementation found) are unrun and **cited,
  not reimplemented** — a badly-tuned baseline is worse than an absent one.
- **Instruct-model collateral (IFEval, COL) unrun.** IFEval requires a
  generation-based verifiable-instruction harness; this pipeline is entirely
  likelihood-based, so COL is a new evaluation stack rather than a run. Collateral
  is MMLU + perplexity only.
- **Acquired-bias arm dropped** by pre-registration (v7 §ACQ) — it belongs to the
  retired science line.

## 10. Relationship to the original thesis

The program's named hypothesis (H2) is that same-family designers fail on
*inherited* bias while matching cross-family on *acquired* bias — design.md §0:
"the whole paper lives or dies on one interaction term."

**Measured: interaction = +0.004 [−0.042, +0.051]** (33 inherited cells, 5
acquired). Same-family designers are not worse on inherited bias
(−0.009 [−0.048, +0.031]). Ten operationalisations failed to locate a family
mechanism (G, O4, K1, W, W′/W″, MV-A, MV-B, MV-C, elicitation-gap,
profile-similarity); designer↔target bias-profile similarity does not predict
removal among non-self designers (+0.220 [−0.736, +0.723], n = 213).

**What survives from the original line, and belongs in this paper:**

- **H1** — within-family co-encoding predicts self-removal, ρ = −0.81 (n = 8).
  Robust to the strongest competing predictor: partial
  r(sharing, self_removal | contrast_gap) = **−0.758** vs raw −0.757.
  This is a *family-level capacity* claim, and it is compatible with H2's null:
  co-encoding predicts how well a family repairs **itself**, without creating a
  relative penalty against outside designers.
- **`contrast_gap` predicts removal** — +0.507 [+0.220, +0.732] (n = 504, 26
  cells), and it **absorbs designer size** (−0.009 [−0.029, +0.015] once gap is
  in the model). Endogenous, per-axis, computed pre-edit at corpus-build time.
  This is a deployment rule: it says *which* source corpus to use before paying
  for any training.
- **The 7–9B tie** (V1 gate, Abstract A) and the durable anti-convergence
  geometry (Δd_base = +0.184 [+0.082, +0.280]) are unaffected by any of the above.

## 11. BL-S / BL-T — the baseline frontier

All methods evaluated **always-on** (intervention active during bias probes, MMLU
and perplexity), strict budget, **equal selection space** (4 configurations each:
the edit's 4 α, steering's 2 layers × 2 strengths, DPO's 4 merge scales,

| phi\|crows | **+0.046** | +0.029 | +0.000 | +0.027 |

- edit − steering = **−0.010 [−0.085, +0.056]** → tie (the pre-registered branch)
- edit − SentenceDebias = **−0.028 [−0.108, +0.046]** → tie
- steering − SentenceDebias = **−0.018 [−0.056, +0.020]** → tie
- edit − DPO = **+0.293 [+0.037, +0.535]** → excludes 0
- steering − DPO = **+0.302 [+0.040, +0.573]** → excludes 0
- SentenceDebias − DPO = **+0.321 [+0.055, +0.581]** → excludes 0

**INLP does not enter as a competitor.** Ported from the same bias-bench repo, it
is the only baseline that **fails its own null**: real − random-subspace =
**+0.025 [−0.070, +0.150]**, covering zero, and on phi it is worse than random
(−0.095, −0.011). It is also the most collaterally expensive method tested
(median ppl ratio 1.083 vs SentenceDebias's 1.005; 49/96 configs in budget). Its
removal is therefore not signal-borne on this setup and it is reported as such,
not omitted.

**Three methods tie on removal** — the sign-only edit, inference-time steering,
and SentenceDebias projection — and all three beat DPO. SentenceDebias passes its
own random-subspace null (+0.308 [+0.087, +0.531]) and is cheap: 88 % of configs
in budget, median ppl ratio 1.005, median ΔMMLU +0.000.

This *sharpens* rather than weakens the contribution. Removal on this signal is
not the scarce thing — three methods from three families achieve it. What differs
is the artifact: steering and SentenceDebias are inference-time hooks that must be
carried by the serving stack and add per-token cost; the edit is a **0.4–1.6 MB
patch that merges into the weights and is then free**. The paper should claim
that, not removal superiority.

**Steering passes its own nulls decisively** (real − partition = +0.298
[+0.183, +0.413]; real − random-direction at matched norm = +0.297
[+0.180, +0.415]), so the tie is a real contest, not a vacuous one. Steering is
also cheap: 93 % of its primary configs sit in budget, median ppl ratio 1.010.

**Why DPO fails, and the limit that explains it.** `elicit_selfdebias` chooses
between the same two candidate continuations under the biased and the debiased
prompt, so when a model cannot articulate a contrast it returns the **identical
string** in both arms. Measured across designers, only **8–54 %** of pairs
differ — for `gemma_3b|occ_gender`, 92 % of the "contrastive" corpus is the same
text twice. The differing fraction correlates **+0.79** with `contrast_gap`,
which gives that predictor a mechanical reading: *approximately the fraction of
probe items on which the model can express any contrast*. A direction contrast
can extract signal from a minority of differing items; a preference objective
largely cannot. So the honest claim is **not** "DPO is a weak method" but
"preference training is a poor use of *this* signal, because the signal is sparse
in exactly the way DPO needs it to be dense." DPO nonetheless **wins on
qwen|crows** (+0.277 where the edit is −0.071).

## 11b. BL-T2 — recommended sign source: `sign(Δ_fp)`

design.md offers two sign sources: `sign(Δ_fp)` (take the sign of the
full-precision contrast) and **STE** (train the sign pattern against the debias
objective with a straight-through estimator). Every result in this paper uses the
former; v7 §BL-T2 required the old "STE best" figure to be re-established on the
frozen protocol or retired.

Paired on identical trained contrast vectors, corpora, seeds and budget (2 targets
× 2 axes × 3 seeds), with the STE latent **initialised at the contrast vector** so
both variants start from the same point:

**STE − sign(Δ_fp) = −0.0074 [−0.023, +0.001]**, n = 12 — covers zero.

Ten of twelve cells agree within ±0.006; the one material difference is
`gemma|crows` seed 1, where STE collapsed to 0.000 against sign_fp's +0.093.

**Recommendation: `sign(Δ_fp)` is the default.** Training the sign pattern buys
nothing measurable while adding a training stage, a learning rate, a step budget
and an extra failure mode. **The old "STE best" figure is RETIRED, not
re-established** (see §12), and STE becomes an ablation row. The simplest sign
source is the right one.

## 13. REC — two reconciliations

1. **`contrast_gap` vs K1.** `contrast_gap` predicts removal at the cell/corpus
   level (+0.507 [+0.220, +0.732], n = 504) while the axis-level elicitation-gap
   regression (K1) was null. These are not in conflict: the predictor operates
   **within and across cells**, not on axis aggregates, and §11 gives its
   mechanical reading (the fraction of items on which a contrast is expressible).
2. **`pre_skew` frame vs the retired taxonomy.** This paper adopts the functional
   frame — removal happens where measurable bias exists, with `pre_skew` reported
   beside removal on every axis (§7) — and does **not** use the v4
   "consistent-semantic-direction" taxonomy. That taxonomy and its three refuted
   geometric predictors (W, W′, W″) belong to the companion, if published at all.

## 14. SPLIT — paper shape

**Recommendation: two papers.**

- **Method paper (this document).** C1–C6, prompt baseline, the BL-S/BL-T
  frontier, axis breadth with its limit, SC7, `contrast_gap` as a deployment
  rule, H1 as a family-level *self-repair capacity* analysis, SUP/REC hygiene.
  The contribution is the artifact + protocol, with removal shared with steering.
- **Companion short (negative-results venue).** The null law: H2 interaction
  **+0.004 [−0.042, +0.051]** with ten refuted operationalisations, the durable
  anti-convergence geometry (Δd_base = +0.184), bridge heterogeneity, and the
