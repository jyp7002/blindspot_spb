## Figure 1

Figure 1: What is removed at each stage. A behavioral edit ΔW is stripped of its magnitudes (one sign bit per weight plus one scale per tensor), then of 99% of its coordinates (magnitude top-k); the surviving structure still removes bias and merges into the checkpoint with no serving-time hook. The shipped artifact is the stage-3 patch — there is no runtime hook, no adapter and no decoding-time intervention to serve.

UNIT. All sizes are MiB (bytes / 2^20). The manuscript prints "MB", but the table reproduces only under MiB: decimal MB would be 4.9% larger (computed at build time from the artifact's own max_mb_decimal / max_mib, not typed).

MEASURED STAGES (blue). Sizes come from results/v10/patch_sizes.json in the v9 tree — panels v6trace/ablate and v6trace/ablate_big, each cell a mean of 12 rows, n = 6 targets. "Dense" is the binary s = 0.0 patch at ≤ 1.0007 bits per retained coordinate (one bit per weight plus one per-tensor scale), NOT the full-precision edit. The 1%-sparse payload spans 0.41–1.60 MiB across the 6 targets: the minimum is qwen-3B, the maximum is llama-8B, which is also the target drawn in the callout strip (160.0 MiB dense → 1.60 MiB at 1% of coordinates, 100× smaller).

The plan's phrase "FP32 magnitudes" is supported by no artifact — the edits live in bf16, which is why the stage is labelled bf16 and the arrow says "− magnitudes (15 of 16 bits)" rather than 31 of 32.

The per-condition interval is a v10 addition (registered:false) — v9 bootstrapped paired DIFFERENCES only — and the bootstrap unit is the cell, each itself a mean of 3 seed rows, not 10 independent models.

PROVENANCE AND ITS LIMITS. Size and effect come from different panels (v6trace/ablate, v6trace/ablate_big vs results_v9/v8dec); no artifact gives a size and a removal for the same run, so this is two v9-gated arms sharing one construction — sign(ΔW) at top-|ΔW| coordinates, one scale per tensor, 1% density — and the build verifies at run time that both arms describe the same density (0.01). patch_sizes.json emits only s = 0.0 and s = 0.99, so no intermediate-sparsity size exists for this panel. phi-3.8B's patch covers only the fused qkv_proj and is not commensurable with the other five targets, though it is an endpoint of neither range, so the range itself is unaffected. Five build-time self-checks (range min/max recomputed from the per-target rows against the artifact's summary block, both manuscript-callout reproduction flags, and DEC density == 1 − effective_sparsity) are written into figures/fig1_data.csv, which carries every per-target size and its source key.

## Figure 3

**Figure 3. Sparsity is close to free until a cliff — and at 7–9B the headline rests on one zeroed non-measurement.**

Paired removal-vs-dense Δ under the v9 integer-item collateral gate, against retained coordinate fraction (log, dense at the left). Left panel: the ≤3.8B tier, n = 4 target cells — gemma-2.6B, llama-3.2B, phi-3.8B, qwen-3B. Right panel: the 7–9B tier, n = 2 cells — llama-8B, qwen-7B. "llama" and "qwen" name different models in the two tiers, so parameter counts are given here: the source artifact drops n_params and the bare target keys collide across tiers. phi-3.8B fuses q/k/v into a single qkv_proj and its edit therefore never touches o_proj, so one of the four ≤3.8B cells comes from a different edited-module set than the other three.

One axis only (occ_gender), designers self-matched, 3 seed rows per cell; a cell's value is the mean over its seed rows. The band is a 10k percentile bootstrap that resamples the target CELL, never the seed, so seed-to-seed spread is invisible in it — the small dots are the per-target Δ the band actually resamples. At n = 2 that bootstrap is degenerate: the 7–9B band is literally [min, max] of the two per-target Δ and must not be read as an inferential interval.

Marker encodings are visual honesty, not decoration. Filled = CI excludes 0; open = CI covers 0; open square = the dense reference, Δ ≡ 0 by construction rather than a measured null. Red × = a budget-fail row: a NaN removal that the standing project rule scores 0.0, i.e. not a measurement — these are drawn as a rug at their exact retained fractions so they can never be absorbed into a line. Open grey circles = as-run budget-fails that the v9 gate fix RECOVERED. The grey dashed curve is a sensitivity: every Δ recomputed with the budget-fails DROPPED instead of zeroed.

Budget-fail census: the ≤3.8B tier has none (0 of 96 rows, v9 and as-run), which is why its panel carries no × markers. The 7–9B tier has three (3 of 48), all llama-8B seed 2, at 100%, 50% and 10% retained — and one of them is inside the dense reference, so every 7–9B Δ is measured against a reference deflated by a non-measurement. Dropping those rows instead of zeroing them turns the registered "sparse beats dense" results at 95%/99%/99.5% sparsity (+0.111/+0.112/+0.131) into −0.011/−0.010/+0.009. That is the grey dashed curve; it is a v10 quantity, recomputed here with the project's own paired bootstrap, and registered in no v9 artifact.

Cliff (the shaded region, labelled "cliff" in each panel) = the retained fractions whose Δ is negative with a CI excluding 0: ≤3.8B from ≥99.5% sparsity (−0.226 [−0.285, −0.155] at 99.5%; −0.417 [−0.513, −0.331] at 99.9%), and 7–9B only at 99.9% (−0.074 [−0.121, −0.027]).

Nor is the dense 7–9B edit "too disruptive to deploy at all": it passes the collateral budget in 5 of 6 runs (llama-8B 2/3, qwen-7B 3/3).

Secondary (top) axis: patch payload size in MiB (bytes/2²⁰; the manuscript prints these as "MB"), shown as the min–max RANGE over that tier's targets, because patch size is per-target and no single MiB value validly describes a tier. Ticks read 40.5–108 / 4.05–10.8 / 0.405–1.08 / 0.0408–0.108 MiB at 100/10/1/0.1% retained for ≤3.8B, and 98–160 / 9.8–16 / 0.98–1.6 / 0.0982–0.16 MiB for 7–9B; every per-target size is in figures/fig3_data.csv.
