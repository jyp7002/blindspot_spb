## Experiment A — arm replication (make-or-break) → **the law replicates**

Five target families: Qwen2.5-1.5B, Llama-3.2-1B, SmolLM2-1.7B, Gemma-2-2b,
Phi-3.5-mini. Each with self + same-family sibling designer, a cross-family
designer, the exogenous ground-truth signal, the data-partition null, and the
sign-shuffle null.

   gemma |       [=====o=====]      | +0.212 [+0.114, +0.310]*
   llama | [====|==o========]       | +0.043 [-0.092, +0.178]
     phi |       |    [==o==]       | +0.271 [+0.178, +0.365]*
    qwen |       |[=====o======]    | +0.253 [+0.018, +0.488]*
    smol |       | [===o===]        | +0.151 [+0.046, +0.255]*

| criterion | verdict | value |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** pooled, arm as random effect | **PASS** | +0.196, CI [+0.109, +0.283], p = 9.8e−06 |
| **A2** per-arm replication | **PASS** | positive **5/5**; CI excludes 0 in **4/5** |
| **A3** blind-spot null-equivalence | **FAIL** | at chance in 2/5 (qwen, gemma); need ≥3 |
| **A4** acquired non-specificity | **PASS** | −0.002, CI [−0.127, +0.096] |
| **A_DEMOTE** | **not triggered** | spine = full (c); proceed C → B |
| **SIGN_NULL_FLAT** | flat everywhere run | \|mean\| ≤ 0.009 vs 0.05 threshold |

**What replicated.** The `origin × designer_family` interaction is positive in
*every* arm and significant in four, pooled p ≈ 1e−5 with arm as a random
effect. The MVP's central risk — that the blind spot was a Qwen artifact — is
resolved. A4 is equally important: on the **acquired** row the cross-vs-same
gap is −0.002 (CI covers 0), so this is not a blanket cross-family advantage;
it is specific to *inherited* bias, exactly as the law claims.

**What did not, and how the claim must be scoped.** A3 asked whether
same-family designers are *at chance versus the data-partition null* on
inherited bias. They are in only 2/5 arms (qwen −0.141, gemma +0.147); in
llama, phi and smol they are clearly above null (+0.344, +0.239, +0.319) while
still below cross-family. So the defensible claim is a **relative deficit on
inherited bias**, not "a model's own debias signal is worthless on its own
blind spot". The strong form is Qwen/Gemma-specific and must be reported as
such.

## Experiment C — bias-direction geometry → **the reframe is supported**

Across **13 axes**, 11 models, corrected stereotype-signed profiles:

| axis | same-family `d` | cross-family | differentiation |
|---|---|---|---|
| nat_pk_nl | 0.671 | 0.074 | **+0.597** |
| nat_ng_no | 0.693 | 0.153 | +0.540 |
| nat_bg_be | 0.547 | 0.133 | +0.413 |
| gen_fm | 0.512 | 0.203 | +0.309 |
| hand_lr | 0.499 | 0.256 | +0.243 |
| rel_bu_lu | 0.502 | 0.259 | +0.243 |
| nat_ro_dk | 0.266 | 0.084 | +0.183 |
| nat_mx_ca | 0.478 | 0.296 | +0.181 |
| age_oy | 0.801 | 0.635 | +0.166 |
| hair_rb | 0.839 | 0.694 | +0.145 |
| ses_lw | 0.940 | 0.810 | +0.130 |
| occ_gender | 0.598 | 0.473 | +0.126 |
| wt_os | 0.351 | 0.356 | −0.006 |

**Same-family models share more bias-direction structure than cross-family
models on 12 of 13 axes** (mean differentiation +0.252; Wilcoxon signed-rank
p = 0.00024) — **while being equally biased in magnitude** (occupation→gender
skew is +0.65 to +0.86 in *every* lineage).

### Measurement bug found and fixed (affects the earlier C numbers)

The valence profile originally computed `ll(A) − ll(B)` per item *without
signing by polarity*, so negative- and positive-attribute items contributed
with the same sign. The profile mean was then dominated by the raw frequency
difference between the two group NAMES, and two models could correlate highly
merely by agreeing that "Dutch" is a commoner token than "Pakistani" — i.e.
`d_axis` was partly measuring shared token frequency rather than shared bias
direction. The occupation-gender profile was already stereotype-signed, so the
two axis families were never comparable.

After the fix, `occ_gender` is unchanged (0.599/0.473) as expected, `gen_fm`
moves from 0.613/0.358 to **0.512/0.203** (differentiation +0.255 → **+0.309**),
and — importantly — `d_axis` becomes **uncorrelated with bias magnitude**
(corr +0.234 → **+0.004**), removing a confound in the Experiment B
regressor.

This is the reframe that experiments_v2 §B demanded, and it cleanly separates
the two candidate premises:

- design.md §3's premise (*disjoint lineage ⇒ the cross designer lacks the
  bias*) is **falsified** — magnitude is equal everywhere.
- the transmission-geometry premise (*shared weight subspace ⇒ co-blindness on
  co-inherited axes*, design.md §10) is **supported** — the sharing is in the
  direction structure, not the magnitude.

### Methodological deviation (necessary, documented)

experiments_v2 §B specifies `d_axis` as the mean pairwise cosine of
same-family bias directions **in weight-delta space**. That is not computable:

- same-family siblings differ in width (Qwen-1.5B 1536 vs Qwen-3B 2048;
  Gemma-2b 2304 vs 9b 3584), so their weight deltas share no coordinate space;
- even at equal width, independently trained models are related only up to
  permutation/rotation of hidden units, so a coordinate-wise cosine is ≈0 by
  construction regardless of shared bias — it measures basis mismatch.

`d_axis` is therefore computed as a **basis-independent item-space functional
profile**: each model's per-item signed bias over the shared probe set, with
`d_axis` = mean within-family profile correlation. Two models that encode an
axis the same way are biased on the same items in the same direction,
regardless of architecture. Still strictly pre-edit, so §B's circularity guard
holds. Weight-space cosine is retained only where it is well defined — between
two directions trained on the *same* model.

## Bugs found and fixed this phase

Each would have silently corrupted or aborted results:

1. **`common.free()` never freed anything.** It did `del o` on its own local
   parameter, so the caller's reference kept every designer model resident
   until the *next* `load()` had already allocated — two models live at peak,
   silently, for the entire MVP. Only became fatal when Gemma's 9B sibling
   pushed the peak past 44 GB.
2. **Phi-3 has no `q_proj`/`v_proj`.** It fuses attention into `qkv_proj`, so
   LoRA matched nothing and injection aborted. Added architecture-aware target
   resolution (also applied to the Experiment E baselines).
3. **Gemma-2's 256k vocabulary** (vs Qwen 152k, Phi 32k) makes a batch-64
   float32 logits tensor ≈19 GB inside `logits / final_logit_softcapping`.
   Two OOMs were misattributed to model residency before this was found; the
   arm needs batch 12.
4. **Injection strength is not transferable.** The frozen recipe gave Gemma
   val_skew **+2.41** against Qwen's +0.55 — 4× the removal headroom, which
   would have broken the cross-arm comparison A2 depends on. Added a
   matched-strength injector (recipe fixed, learning rate searched to a target
   band): Gemma +0.617, Phi +0.775, Qwen +0.552, Llama +0.520, SmolLM2 +0.511.
5. A scheduling race that would have started the Gemma arm against the stale
   over-injected checkpoint while its re-injection was still running.

## Experiment D — robustness controls → **both pass, and D1 strengthens the claim**

**D1. Capability-matched designers.** Restricting designers to an MMLU band
makes the interaction *larger*, not smaller:

| designer set | interaction | 95% CI | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| all designers | +0.196 | [+0.107, +0.285] | 1.5e−05 |
| **MMLU-matched (0.50–0.72)** | **+0.227** | [+0.131, +0.324] | **3.8e−06** |

Dropping the two weakest designers (llama1b, smol360m) increases the effect,
so the "cross-family is simply a stronger model" confound was working
*against* the result. Together with the bidirectional flip, that objection is
now closed from two independent directions.

**D2. Subspace separability (design.md §12).** cosine(inherited direction,
acquired direction) on the *same* target = **+0.0016, 95% CI
[−0.0009, +0.0040]** over 81 pairs, stable across every designer role and arm.

The two biases are effectively orthogonal. This is the mechanistic explanation
for A4: a designer can be blind on the inherited axis while retaining full
leverage on the acquired one *because the two live in disjoint subspaces*.
(This is the one cross-direction cosine that is well defined, since both
directions are trained on the same model and share a basis.)

## Experiment B — the reframe test → **not testable with this axis inventory**

K=7 axes were swept (2 arms × 3 seeds), spanning d_axis 0.09→0.92.

| criterion | verdict | value |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** geometry slope | **FAIL** | slope +0.137, CI [−0.301, +0.574], p=0.54 (raw outcome: +0.060, p=0.65) |
| **B2** magnitude drop-in | **FAIL** | d with magnitude +0.021 (p=0.91); magnitude alone +0.878 (p=0.0008) |
| **B3** acquired control | **PASS** | +0.024, CI [−0.009, +0.080] (covers 0, as predicted) |

The slope is in the **predicted positive direction** on both the normalised
and raw outcome, but nowhere near significant.

**This is an underpowered test, not evidence against the mechanism.** After the
signing fix, `d_axis` is *uncorrelated* with bias magnitude (+0.004), so the
regressor is clean and spans 0.27→0.94. The blocker is now purely the outcome:

| axis | d_axis | pre-bias | removable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| nat_ro_dk | 0.266 | 0.015 | no |
| wt_os | 0.351 | 0.024 | no |
| rel_bu_lu | 0.502 | 0.049 | no |
| **gen_fm** | 0.512 | **0.629** | yes |
| **occ_gender** | 0.598 | **0.860** | yes |
| nat_pk_nl | 0.671 | 0.004 | no |
| age_oy | 0.801 | 0.070 | marginal |
| ses_lw | 0.940 | 0.108 | marginal |

Only **two** axes carry enough systematic bias for removal to mean anything,
and one of those (gen_fm) is the injected axis whose blind-spot magnitude is
~0 by construction (B3). A regression over 22 rows with 2 informative points
cannot resolve a slope. B2's "magnitude predicts" result is largely a
normalisation artifact: b_norm = b_raw / pre_bias, and pre_bias correlates
+0.744 with the magnitude covariate.

**What B requires** is an axis inventory with *high removable bias across a
range of direction-sharing*. Note this cannot be manufactured by injection:
B3 confirms injected axes have b ≈ 0 regardless of d, so they carry no signal
for the B1 slope. The tractable route is a BBQ-derived probe family — 9
categories with genuinely strong measured bias and a uniform item structure
(context + two group answers) that yields both a continuous per-item profile
for d_axis and natural minimal pairs for elicitation.

Note the mechanism is *not* unsupported overall: Experiment C shows
same-family models share more bias-direction structure than cross-family ones
on both measurable axes, and D2 shows inherited/acquired occupy disjoint
subspaces. What B was meant to add is the *dose-response* — that more sharing
means a bigger blind spot — and that specific claim remains untested.

## Experiment E — safety net + systems corollary

### E2: the full baseline suite at matched collateral

Definitive comparison: Qwen arm, identical configuration space (4 α per
method), all 18 (seed × origin × designer) cells, and **a cell where a method
cannot operate inside the collateral budget scores 0** rather than being
dropped.

| method | dBias (all cells) | cells operable |
|---|---|---|
| **STE-trained signs** | **+0.400** | 89% |
| PCGU | +0.336 | 89% |
| binary sign(Δ_fp) | +0.334 | 78% |
| full-precision task vector | +0.259 | 56% |
| DPO | +0.176 | 67% |
| activation steering | +0.174 | 50% |
| FairLoRA | +0.168 | 83% |
| sign-shuffle null | +0.004 | 50% |

Two findings, both of which required getting the comparison protocol right:

1. **Binary beats full precision at matched collateral** (+0.334 vs +0.259).
   Not merely "binarization is ~free" as the MVP concluded — the binary edit is
   *gentler*, so it stays inside the collateral budget in 78% of cells against
   full precision's 56%. Its advantage is collateral, not raw bias removal.
2. **E1 answer, reversed from an earlier reading: STE-trained signs DO buy
   something** over direct `sign(Δ_fp)` — +0.400 vs +0.334 (~20% better) and
   operable in 89% of cells vs 78%. Learning the sign pattern against the
   debias objective is worth its cost.

### Three protocol errors that each inverted the ranking

Recorded because each produced a confident and wrong answer:

1. **Mis-scaled baselines.** DPO/FairLoRA/PCGU return a trained update whose
   α=1 already means "merge the adapter"; sweeping them over the task-vector
   grid {2,4,8,16} applied 2–16× that update. DPO's median perplexity ratio was
   **33**, FairLoRA's **6811**; they met the collateral budget in 1.4% and
   **0%** of runs. PCGU had the opposite fault (too weak: ppl ratio 1.000,
   dBias ≈ 0). Comparing our method against baselines crippled this way would
   have manufactured a win. Re-run at merge-scale α (≤1) and, for PCGU, a
   stronger optimisation budget.
2. **Unequal selection space.** The binary edit was being chosen from 6–11
   configurations per cell (its sparsity variants from the E3 sweep sat in the
   same file) while every baseline had 4. More configurations to maximise over
   is a free advantage.
3. **Survivorship in the frontier average.** Averaging each method only over
   the cells where it found an in-budget point rewards methods that fail more
   often — full precision looked best (+0.466) precisely *because* it operated
   in only 56% of cells. Scoring budget failure as 0 is what makes the
   comparison mean anything.

### E3: bit-budget frontier

Sparsity sweep {0, 0.5, 0.9, 0.99, 0.999} across the qwen and llama arms is in
`results/runs_expE.jsonl` (mode `bit_budget`). MVP finding stands: 90% sparsity
(~963 KB) matches the dense edit; 99% (~96 KB) retains ~62%.

**Not implemented:** E4 (in-place byte-patch on a quantized checkpoint).

## Experiment B, second attempt — CrowS-Pairs axis family

The first attempt was underpowered because only 2 of 8 axes carried removable
bias. CrowS-Pairs was added as a new axis family precisely to fix that: 7 axes
with real exhibited bias (|skew| 0.06–0.40), native minimal pairs, disjoint
probe/edit halves. K=8 with occupation-gender. 1680 new records
(7 axes × 2 arms × 3 seeds).

**B1 still fails**: slope +0.038, CI [−0.319, +0.395], p = 0.83 (n=36).
**B2 fails**: d with magnitude −0.162 (p=0.34); magnitude alone +0.603
(p=0.006). **B3 passes**.

### The reason is a substantive limitation of the METHOD, not of the axes

The new axes have plenty of bias; what they lack is *removability*. Fraction of
each axis's bias actually removed, at the strict collateral budget:

| axis | pre-bias | same | cross | exogenous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| socioeconomic | 0.191 | 0.149 | 0.261 | **0.510** |
| physical-appearance | 0.262 | 0.076 | 0.133 | **0.346** |
| religion | 0.137 | 0.114 | 0.229 | −0.160 |
| race-color | 0.024 | 0.238 | 0.089 | 0.145 |
| age | 0.139 | 0.071 | 0.002 | −0.024 |
| sexual-orientation | 0.146 | 0.045 | 0.009 | 0.040 |
| disability | 0.381 | 0.021 | 0.028 | 0.075 |

Compare occupation-gender, where the same pipeline removes **24–75%**.

Low-rank binary edits on q/v projections transfer well when the bias is
*templated* — occupation-gender's probe and edit corpora share sentence frames,
so a compact direction generalises across held-out occupations. CrowS items are
heterogeneous natural sentences on unrelated topics, and a single low-rank
direction learned on one disjoint half removes little of the bias measured on
the other. With almost no removal to differentiate, (cross − same) is noise.

### Consequence: the blind spot does NOT replicate on CrowS

| | mean (cross − same) | 95% CI | P(>0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| CrowS, pooled over 7 axes (n=42) | **+0.0052** | [−0.0037, +0.0153] | 0.853 |

By axis, the gap is positive exactly on the three axes where removal is
non-trivial (socioeconomic +0.022, religion +0.016, physical-appearance +0.015)
and noise on the four where it is not (age −0.010, sexual-orientation −0.005,
race-color −0.004, disability +0.003).

**This is the most important scope limit in the programme.** The blind-spot law
of Experiment A is established on *templated* bias probes — the occupation-
gender pronoun probe and the injected valence axis — where the editing method
removes a large fraction of the bias. On a naturalistic stereotype benchmark it
is not detectable, because the method itself removes too little bias there for
any designer difference to show. Whether the law is specific to templated bias,
or merely masked by a weak editing method on naturalistic content, is not
resolved by these data; distinguishing the two requires an editing method that
works on heterogeneous bias (higher rank, more layers, or MLP targets) before
the axis-level dose-response can be tested at all.

## Edit-capacity sweep — the q/v default was the binding constraint

Prompted by B2's apparent failure, edit capacity was swept against the
EXOGENOUS (ground-truth) signal on CrowS disability (pre-bias 0.438). Ground
truth is the ceiling: if it cannot remove a bias at a given capacity, no
designer can, and the designer comparison there is vacuous.

| target set | rank | params | best removal | **in-budget** | fraction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **attn (q,k,v,o)** | **16** | 154M | +0.423 | **+0.421** | **96.1%** |
| attn_mlp | 64 | 1310M | +0.311 | +0.311 | 71.1% |
| mlp | 16 | 1156M | +0.299 | +0.299 | 68.3% |
| attn_mlp | 16 | 1310M | +0.286 | +0.286 | 65.4% |
| qv (frozen default) | 16 | 77M | +0.413 | **none** | — |
| qv | 64 | 77M | +0.386 | none | — |
| attn | 64 | 154M | +0.385 | none | — |
| mlp | 64 | 1156M | +0.314 | none | — |

**Every configuration removes a similar amount of bias unconstrained
(0.29–0.42); what separates them is how much capability they destroy doing
it.** Adding `k_proj` and `o_proj` — a 2× parameter increase — takes CrowS
disability from *zero* in-budget removal to 96%. More capacity is actively
harmful: `attn_mlp` strictly contains `attn` yet scores 65% against 96%, and
rank 64 fails to reach budget in three of four target sets. Attention-only at
rank 16 is the sweet spot.

### This retracts the B2 conclusion

B2 reported that the blind spot "does not replicate on CrowS" and speculated
that naturalistic stereotype bias might be inseparable from capability. Both
were artifacts of the frozen `q_proj + v_proj` target set inherited from the
MVP: with q/v, *no* designer could remove CrowS bias inside the collateral
budget, so every cell scored ~0 and the designer comparison was never actually
exercised. The CrowS designer sweep is being re-run at `--targets q k v o`.

### And it makes the A-series results conditional

Experiments A, C, D and E all used q/v. Their effect sizes are therefore
conditioned on a target set now known to be suboptimal. Two complete A arms
(qwen, phi) are being re-run at attention targets to check the
`origin × designer_family` interaction survives; the q/v baselines to beat are
qwen +0.253 [+0.018, +0.488] and phi +0.271 [+0.178, +0.365].

### Scope boundary for the whole programme

Putting A and B3 together:

- On **templated** bias -- the occupation-gender pronoun probe and the injected
  valence axis, where probe and edit corpora share sentence frames -- the
  `origin x designer_family` blind spot replicates across all five model
  families (pooled +0.196, p ~ 1e-5).
- On **naturalistic** stereotype content (CrowS), *every* endogenous designer
  fails, same-family and cross-family alike, while the exogenous signal still
  works.

So the law as stated is specific to probe-structured bias. On naturalistic
content the result is different and in one sense stronger: endogenous
self-debiasing fails across the board rather than selectively. Any claim about
"self-repair having a blind spot" must be scoped to the former; the latter is a
broader negative result about endogenous elicitation itself.

## Cross-family panel — is it the RELATION, or the specific designer?

For three of five arms the cross designer was always llama3b, so "cross beats
same" could be "llama3b is a good debiaser". This rotates the cross designer
through all four other families for two targets (qwen, phi), templated axes,
q/v, 3 seeds, pooling with the existing cross records.

Per-family cross-advantage over same-family on INHERITED bias, pooled over both
targets and seeds:

| cross family | mean advantage | 95% CI | P(>0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **llama** | +0.275 | [+0.257, +0.291] | 1.00 |
| **gemma** | +0.181 | [+0.103, +0.248] | 1.00 |
| **phi** | +0.141 | [+0.021, +0.256] | 1.00 |
| qwen | −0.028 | [−0.342, +0.304] | 0.38 |
| smollm | −0.053 | [−0.257, +0.167] | 0.31 |
| **ALL** | **+0.115** | [+0.023, +0.197] | 0.99 |

Per target (inherited), families beating same-family: qwen target 3/4,
phi target 3/4. The one dramatic failure is smol1.7b→qwen (+0.083 vs same
+0.373, i.e. −0.290) — but smol1.7b is not a weak designer in general: it
removes +0.522 as cross for phi and +0.360 as its own same-family. The failure
is in the smol→qwen *pairing*, not the model.

**This qualifies the law.** "Cross-family succeeds" is too strong. What holds:

1. Same-family designers reliably underperform on inherited bias (the blind
   spot proper) — stable across the whole programme.
2. Cross-family designers beat same-family *on average* (+0.115, CI excludes 0),
   which is why the A interaction replicated.
3. But it is NOT a clean relational guarantee: there is a stable
   designer-quality ordering (llama > gemma > phi > {qwen, smollm}) that holds
   regardless of target, and the two weakest cross families do not beat
   same-family at all.

**It also recalibrates the A-series headline.** llama3b was the constant cross
designer on three of five arms, and the panel shows it is the *strongest*
debias source (+0.275). So the A interaction (+0.196) was real but flattered by
that choice; +0.115 is the honest cross-family-*average* advantage. On the
ACQUIRED axis every family clusters within ±0.05 of same-family (A4 confirmed
per-family), so the asymmetry remains inherited-specific.

## Full designer x target panel (5x5) — the law decomposes cleanly

All five targets x all five designer families, inherited bias, templated axis,
q/v, 3 seeds. Removal (rows = target family, cols = designer family; diagonal
is self-family):

| target / designer | qwen | llama | gemma | phi | smollm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **qwen** | *0.373* | 0.649 | 0.603 | 0.514 | 0.083 |
| **llama** | 0.555 | *0.651* | 0.731 | 0.607 | 0.299 |
| **gemma** | 0.641 | 0.615 | *0.207* | 0.006 | 0.477 |
| **phi** | 0.310 | 0.611 | 0.469 | *0.338* | 0.522 |
| **smollm** | 0.609 | 0.608 | 0.681 | 0.487 | *0.390* |

The blind spot decomposes into two additive, separable effects:

**1. Self-penalty (the relational law).** Off-diagonal mean 0.504 vs diagonal
0.392. Per target x seed: **+0.112, 95% CI [+0.044, +0.177], P(>0)=1.000**.
Holds for 4 of 5 targets; the exception is llama (−0.103 — its own family
debiases it slightly better than cross designers). So "a target's own family is
the worst at debiasing it" is a strong tendency, not a universal.

**2. Designer quality (a nuisance term, but a real one).** Column means give a
stable ordering — gemma ≈ llama (0.62) > qwen (0.53) > phi (0.40) > smollm
(0.35) — visible in nearly every row. smollm is the weakest designer against
every target (0.08–0.52), which is what dragged down the cross-family average
in the two-target panel.

**This is the cleanest statement of the mechanism the programme produced.** The
A-series interaction (+0.196) was the SUM of these two effects, and it was
inflated because llama3b (a top designer) was the constant cross designer on
three of five arms. The part that is genuinely about the same/cross *relation*
— the self-penalty — is **+0.112**, and that is the defensible core of the law.
The designer-quality span (~0.27) is orthogonal to it and must be controlled
(capability-matching, D1, only partly does so — the ordering is not pure MMLU:
gemma2b at MMLU 0.52 is a top designer).

## Experiment F — what drives the two terms of the decomposition?

### Designer quality is the ELICITATION GAP, and nothing else

Regressing a designer family's quality (its mean off-diagonal removal) on four
candidate properties, over 60 cells:

| predictor | Pearson r | p |
|---|---|---|
| **elicitation gap** | **+0.339** | **0.008** |
| residual bias (occ_skew) | +0.082 | 0.53 |
| MMLU (capability) | −0.089 | 0.50 |
| size | +0.131 | 0.32 |

A model is a good debias-signal source exactly to the extent that it can
separate biased from debiased text on request (its Schick-style self-diagnosis
contrast) -- **not** its capability (MMLU is null and negatively signed;
gemma2b at MMLU 0.52 is a top designer, phi3.5 at 0.66 is middling), not its
residual bias, not its size. This is why D1's capability-matching only partly
controlled the nuisance term: capability is not the nuisance, elicitation gap
is. It is also mechanistically clean -- the whole pipeline runs on the
designer's elicited contrast, so the quality of that contrast is what carries
through.

### The self-penalty tracks within-family bias-direction sharing

Per-family within-family sharing of the occupation-gender bias direction
(item-profile correlation among that family's siblings) vs the family's
own-target removal:

| family | within-family sharing | self-removal |
|---|---|---|
| smollm | 0.346 | 0.390 |
| llama | 0.565 | 0.651 |
| qwen | 0.608 | 0.373 |
| gemma | 0.661 | 0.207 |
| phi | 0.794 | 0.338 |

Spearman rho = **−0.80, p=0.10** (n=5): the more a family's siblings encode the
bias the same way, the less its own family can remove it -- Experiment C's
co-blindness at the family level. Underpowered (n=5, not significant on its
own), but it (a) matches the direction of the strong axis-level result (C:
12/13 axes, p=0.0002) and (b) resolves the panel's one anomaly: **llama has the
lowest within-family sharing (0.565) and is the single family with no
self-penalty** (−0.10) and the highest self-removal (0.651). The mechanism
predicts llama should be least blind to its own bias, and it is on all three
measures at once.

### Bottom line

The blind spot = (a) a **geometric self-penalty** — a designer cannot remove a
bias it co-encodes with its target, scaling with within-family direction
sharing — plus (b) a **designer-quality** nuisance set entirely by the
designer's elicitation gap. Reported separately, both are interpretable; the
A-series interaction was their sum.
