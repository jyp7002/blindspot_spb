### G (mediation) — FAIL: the self-penalty is NOT pairwise direction co-encoding

Model over the 30 panel cells (designer checkpoint × target × seed):

|---|---|---|---|
| d_ij (sharing) | +0.125 | [−0.201, +0.451] | 0.45 |
| elicit gap | +0.052 | [−0.412, +0.516] | 0.83 |

- **G1 FAIL**: the sharing coefficient is not negative-and-significant; it is
  ns with the wrong sign.
- **G2 FAIL (primary)**: `same` stays significant *with* d_ij in the model
  (p=0.026); the bootstrap indirect effect (same→d→removal) CI covers 0.
- **G3 FAIL**: d(smol1.7b, qwen1.5b)=+0.52 is *below* the cross-family 75th

### M redesigned (v2) — confounds fixed; frame-overlap REFUTED as the driver

The two v1 confounds were fixed and validated by a gate that measures
bias-preservation + minimal-pair integrity BEFORE the designer sweep:
- de-templatize now paraphrases ONCE with pronoun placeholders (minimal pairs
  preserved), and
- templatize now extracts a SHORT adjectival trait into a fixed slot
  ("People often say that the {group} is {trait}"), genuinely valence-like.

Gate outcome (measured on qwen1.5b): only **mt_crows_socioeconomic** passed
clean (pre-bias 0.146 preserved, minimal pairs valid, 57 items). religion/age
failed on minimal-pair integrity / item floor; **md_occ_gender REJECTED** —
de-templatizing still dropped the exhibited bias 0.69 → 0.154 even with minimal
pairs intact. That rejection is itself a finding: **the occupation-gender bias
is substantially carried by the templated frame** — paraphrase the frame and
the bias weakens, independent of content.

**M1 — clean NEGATIVE.** On the one clean axis, templatizing (shared frame,
group→stereotype content and bias preserved) did NOT raise removability:
exogenous ceiling 0.024 → 0.003. Creating frame overlap does not make a
naturalistic axis removable.

**Refined mechanism.** The scope boundary is therefore NOT "frame overlap".
The templatized CrowS shares a frame but has HETEROGENEOUS group contrasts
(garbage-man/lawyer, poor/rich, secretary/boss, …), each appearing once —
whereas the removable axes (occ_gender: he/she; gen_fm: F/M) repeat ONE
discriminative contrast across every item, so a single edit direction
generalizes. The refined hypothesis: **removability requires a REPEATED
discriminative contrast, not just a repeated frame.** A quick single-contrast
probe (low-income/wealthy with CrowS-derived traits) was confounded by weak
bias (0.037, mixed-polarity traits) and is only suggestive; a clean test of
contrast-repetition (one strong contrast × many traits, polarity-controlled)
is the remaining open experiment.

**Net M verdict:** the simple frame-overlap/structural hypothesis is REFUTED;
the boundary is governed by discriminative-contrast repetition (and bias
strength). This is a sharper, more mechanistic account than v2's descriptive
"templated vs naturalistic", and it is a clean result rather than the v1
confound.

# v3 SUMMARY — where the programme stands

**Mechanism (nailed):** the self-penalty scales with within-family bias-
direction co-encoding — H1: Spearman ρ=−0.81, p=0.015, n=8 families, CI
excludes 0. This is the paper's central law.

**Config-fragility (characterized):** the clean 5/5 pooled *interaction* was
q/v-specific; at the more capable attn edit it is null (L), because it mixes the
robust self-penalty with a cross-designer-quality term. Report the mechanism,
not the interaction.

**Pairwise vs aggregate (G):** the self-penalty is a family-lineage AGGREGATE
property, not predicted by any single designer-target pairwise correlation.

**Scope boundary (refined, M):** NOT frame-overlap (refuted — templatizing does
not restore removability). The boundary is governed by discriminative-CONTRAST
repetition: the edit removes bias only where one contrast repeats across items.
Two open confirmations remain: a polarity-controlled single-vs-many-contrast
test, and a group-vs-unknown BBQ onboarding.

**Two-factor → refined model:** removal = f(designer elicitation gap [quality],
within-family co-encoding [self-penalty], contrast-repetition [removability]).

## Contrast-repetition CONFIRMED (on natural bias) — the M follow-ups

Two confirmatory experiments for M's refined hypothesis (removability requires a
REPEATED discriminative contrast, not a shared frame):

### CONFIRM-1: BBQ group-vs-unknown — PASS

The BBQ minimal pairs were rebuilt as group-vs-UNKNOWN (the real BBQ bias:
preferring a stereotyped group over "unknown" in ambiguous contexts) instead of
group-A-vs-group-B. Same benchmark, same content; the only change is that the
discriminative contrast (toward-unknown) now REPEATS across items.

| BBQ axis | pre-bias | exo | same | cross | null | group-A-vs-B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Age | 0.382 | 0.074 | 0.291 | 0.255 | 0.003 | ~0 (unremovable) |
| Race | 0.371 | 0.055 | 0.275 | 0.161 | 0.227 | ~0 |
| Religion | 0.294 | 0.019 | 0.275 | 0.214 | 0.007 | ~0 |
| Gender | 0.171 | 0.020 | 0.113 | 0.122 | 0.011 | ~0 |

group-A-vs-B BBQ exhibited ~0 bias and ~0 removal; group-vs-unknown exhibits
0.17-0.38 bias and is REMOVABLE (66-94% of same-family; above-null removal
+0.10 to +0.29 for Age/Religion/Gender; Race is mostly null). Same content,
different contrast structure => removability. **This confirms contrast-repetition
on natural benchmark bias.** (Caveat: endogenous > exogenous here, and the Race
null is high (0.23) -- part of the removal is "any direction in the toward-
unknown subspace", as with the gender minimal-pair null.)

### CONFIRM-2: injection-controlled single-vs-many

v1 was confounded (unregularised injection blew perplexity 39-65 -> nothing
in-budget). Rerun with the frozen regularisation recipe (WikiText-train mixed
in) keeps perplexity ~13 (in budget), bias matched:

| condition | injected skew | best in-budget removal | fraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| single (1 contrast)  | 0.657 | 0.513 | 78% |
| many (10 contrasts)  | 0.579 | 0.385 | 67% |

Single-contrast removes MORE than many (78% vs 67%), directionally consistent
with contrast-repetition. But the separation is MODEST (11 pts) and within the
noise of the alpha sweep at one seed (single trace 0.45/0.51/0.30/0.36/0.59) --
NOT the dramatic single-removable/many-not the natural data shows. Reading:
**injection installs a partly-coherent direction removable even across 10
contrasts**, so it attenuates the effect. Injection is an attenuated instrument;
the strong clean confirmation is CONFIRM-1 (natural bias, decisive flip).

### Net

The scope boundary is contrast-repetition, confirmed on natural bias (BBQ): a
low-rank edit removes bias where ONE discriminative contrast repeats across
items (occ_gender he/she; BBQ toward-unknown) and fails where the contrast
varies per item (CrowS; BBQ group-A-vs-B). Frame-overlap is refuted (M); bias
magnitude is not the driver (BBQ group-A-vs-B had ~0 bias BECAUSE the contrast
was non-repeating). This is the mechanistic replacement for v2's descriptive
"templated vs naturalistic" scope statement.

## Refined scope law (supersedes "contrast-repetition")

Removability requires a **consistent semantic bias-direction** across items:
- REMOVABLE: occ_gender (pronoun), gen_fm (F/M), BBQ toward-unknown, StereoSet
  (stereo→anti) — all have one coherent direction, even when surface tokens or
  attributes vary.
- NOT REMOVABLE: CrowS at any K, BBQ group-A-vs-B — heterogeneous group→content
  associations with no single semantic direction; frame-templatising (M) or
  repeating the surface contrast (S) does not create one.

Neither surface frame-overlap (M, refuted) nor surface contrast-repetition
(S, refuted) is the driver; the driver is whether the bias forms a single
coherent direction the low-rank edit can capture. This is the v4 closure of the
scope section.

## Why (this is a design limitation, not a clean refutation)
1. **Removable-axis ceiling.** occ_gender is highly removable; at 7–9B the

2. **self ≥ sibling for every measurable family.** The self-designer (elicited
   on the *exact* target) removes MORE than the smaller sibling — best-matched
   corpus wins. Both are within-family, so neither exposes the self-penalty,
   which is defined relative to CROSS-family designers.
3. **No cross-family designer at T2.** run_t2 only ran self + sibling (both
   within-family). The proper self-penalty (cross − same) is therefore not
   computable from this run — the sharing→self-removal proxy is all we have, and
   it is underpowered.
4. **Two families collateral-infeasible.** llama-self is infeasible while its
   sibling works (self edit is more collaterally destructive — a *self-specific*
   penalty signal, but n = 1); granite is infeasible for both roles (family-level
   fragility, uninformative).

## What this does and doesn't say
- It does **not** show the co-encoding→self-penalty mechanism breaking at scale —
  the design can't see it here (ceiling + no cross designers).
- It does show that on a *removable* inherited axis, 7–9B self-designers are good
  enough to remove most of the bias, so any residual self-penalty is small
  relative to edit capacity.
- The definitive T2 test still needs: **cross-family designers at 7–9B** (for the
  real cross − same self-penalty) on a **non-ceiling (naturalistic) axis**. Logged
  as the open follow-up. Data: `results/h1_at_t2.json`.

# v4 — CROSS-DESIGNER T2 (definitive self-penalty test): BLIND SPOT DOES NOT SURVIVE TO 7–9B

Full cross-family panel on the 96GB GPU (completed in one session, 60 rows = 5
targets × {self, sibling, 4 cross} × 2 axes × 1 seed). Each target's 7–9B model
is debiased by its own elicitation (self), its smaller sibling, and every OTHER
family's 7–9B model (cross). Corpora pre-elicited once and cached.

## Self-removal at 7–9B is high, and the SELF-designer is the BEST debiaser

occ_gender in-budget removal (self / mean-cross):
| target | self | mean cross | cross − self |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen  | 0.677 | 0.653 | −0.024 |
| llama | 0.751 | 0.661 | −0.090 |
| gemma | 0.808 | 0.793 | −0.015 |
| olmo  | 0.832 | 0.759 | −0.074 |

**Mean cross − self = −0.051 (occ), −0.048 (crows): a self-*advantage*, not a
self-penalty.** A 7–9B model debiases ITSELF better than any other family
debiases it — its own elicitation is the best-matched corpus. On crows the effect
is sharpest for gemma (self 0.150 vs mean-cross 0.011). granite is collateral-
infeasible (all nan) and is excluded.

## The blind spot, measured the same way as the small-scale law, is GONE

corr(within-family sharing, SELF-removal) — identical metric to the small-scale
H1 (ρ = −0.81, p = 0.015, n = 8):
- occ_gender:  Spearman ρ = **−0.20** (p = 0.80, n = 4)
- crows_socioeconomic: ρ = +0.20 (p = 0.80)

The −0.81 relationship attenuates to null. Self-removal no longer declines with
within-family co-encoding.

## The one surviving correlation is a sibling-size artifact
The {self, sibling} "same-family" penalty (cross − same) DOES correlate with
sharing on occ (Spearman ρ = +0.80, p ≈ 0.10–0.20). But this is driven entirely
by the same-family SIBLING being a weak designer (llama_sib removal 0.239,
gemma_sib 0.571, vs their self 0.751 / 0.808). Drop the sibling and use self-only
and the correlation collapses (ρ = +0.40, p = 0.60) while the penalty reverses to
a self-advantage. The sibling is a smaller model, so this is designer *size/quality*,
not co-encoding. On the naturalistic crows axis even this vanishes (ρ = −0.10).

## Interpretation — durable geometry, non-durable blind spot
Reconciles with O1: the family bias-DIRECTION signature is durable to 27–32B
(Δd 0.125), but the self-repair BLIND SPOT (co-encoding → can't self-remove) is
**scale-scoped and gone by 7–9B**. Distinct family directions persist, yet by
7–9B a model has enough edit capacity + self-matched elicitation to overcome any
co-encoding disadvantage and repair itself — better, in fact, than out-family
designers. **The self-repair blind spot is a small-model phenomenon.** Data:
`results/t2x/removal.jsonl`, `results/t2x_selfpenalty_*.json`.

Caveats: n = 4 clean families (granite infeasible), 1 seed; the cross designers
are all 7–9B while the sibling is 3B/2b (size not fully matched); a fully clean
co-encoding test would size-match designers. But the headline — self is the best
debiaser and the ρ = −0.81 attenuates to −0.20 — is robust across both framings.

# v5 W-line CONCLUSION — removability is FUNCTIONAL, not geometric (three refutations)

W'' (high-rank natural-rank test): trained the ground-truth ΔW at rank 128
(8× the edit rank) on qwen1.5b. Natural participation-ratio effective rank is
**uniformly 1.8-2.3 across ALL 31 axes** (e16 ~0.99 = 99% energy in the top 16 of
128 dims). Every bias direction — removable (occ peff 1.86 / removal 0.640; gen_fm
1.84 / 0.497; crows_disability 1.99 / 0.421) and non-removable (crows_race 1.85 /
0.013; age_oy 1.80 / 0.014) alike — is intrinsically ~rank-2. W'' r=−0.15 (null).

**Combined verdict across three de-circularization attempts:**
| test | operationalization | result |
|---|---|---|
| W | activation-diff top-1 PCA EVR | FAIL (r=−0.37, tracks template-homogeneity) |
| W' | weight ΔW rank-16 concentration | FAIL (peff ~2 uniform) |
| W'' | weight ΔW natural rank (r128) | FAIL (peff ~2 uniform) |

**Removability is not a geometric property of the bias direction.** Every social
bias forms a low-rank (~2), coherent weight direction a rank-16 edit can fully
represent. The removable/non-removable split is FUNCTIONAL: whether negating the
elicited edit-half direction transfers to reduce the PROBE-half bias within the
collateral budget. Geometry is necessary-but-not-sufficient (all axes have a
coherent direction; only some are removable). This PREEMPTS the circularity
objection by bounding what removability is NOT — the paper states the scope law as
a behavioral/functional taxonomy, with three geometric predictors tested and
refuted, rather than leaving the circularity merely acknowledged. Data:
results/evr_removability.json, evr_weight.json, evr_weight_r128.json.
