# v4 scale phase — summary

Three experiments took the self-repair blind-spot program to scale (27–72B
designers, 7–9B self-repair). One sentence: **the family bias geometry is
durable at scale, but the self-repair blind spot is not — it is a small-model
phenomenon.**

---

## O1 — the family bias-direction signature is durable to 27–32B

Item-space bias profiles for two bf16 large models (gemma-2-27b, Qwen2.5-32B)
against the ≤9B tiers, 6-axis basis (occ + 5 CrowS). Δd = same-family −
cross-family profile correlation.

| tier | same_d | cross_d | Δd |
|---|---|---|---|
| <3.5B | 0.634 | 0.476 | 0.157 |
| 27–32B | 0.655 | 0.530 | **0.125** |

The signature does **not** converge away: gemma27b's Δd (0.159) is as strong at
27B as at small scale, and same-family lineage is if anything tighter (gemma27b
vs gemma9b = 0.738). Corrects the earlier extrapolation (which read Δd→0 from
noisy single-axis data). **Distinct family bias directions persist at scale.**

## R2 — a bigger designer does not beat the removability ceiling

Pre-registered falsifiable prediction: a large designer does not exceed the
exogenous (ground-truth-corpus) removal ceiling on naturalistic CrowS. Trained
the small-target edit from qwen32b's elicited corpora and compared to ground
truth on the same target/axis/config:

| axis | qwen32b designer | exogenous ground truth | margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| crows_socioeconomic | +0.105 | +0.108 | **−0.003 → PASS** |

Designer scale **matches but does not substitute for** ground truth. The
removability of naturalistic bias is set by the axis, not by designer capability.
(Caveat: n=1 designer, 32B not 70B — the strict 70B claim is untested by choice.)

## T2 — the self-repair blind spot is gone by 7–9B

Full cross-family panel: each target's 7–9B model debiased by self, sibling, and
every other family's 7–9B model. Two results, one metric.

**The self-designer is the best debiaser** (self-*advantage*, not penalty):

| axis | mean(cross − self) |
|---|---|
| occ_gender | −0.051 |
| crows_socioeconomic | −0.048 |

A 7–9B model debiases itself better than any other family does — its own
elicitation is the best-matched corpus (sharpest: gemma removes crows 0.150 with
its own corpus vs 0.011 cross).

**Measured identically to the small-scale law**, corr(within-family sharing,
self-removal) attenuates from **ρ = −0.81 (p=0.015, n=8)** to **ρ = −0.20**
(occ, n=4). The relationship vanishes. The one correlation that looked like
survival (sharing vs {self+sibling} penalty, ρ=+0.80 on occ) is a sibling-*size*
artifact — the same-family sibling is a smaller, weaker designer; self-only
collapses it to ρ=+0.40 and reverses the sign, and it is absent on the
naturalistic axis.

---

## Synthesis

| property | small (<3.5B) | scale |
|---|---|---|
| family bias-direction geometry | distinct (Δd 0.157) | **durable** to 27–32B (Δd 0.125) |
| naturalistic-bias removability | axis-bounded | **axis-bounded**, designer scale doesn't help (R2) |
| self-repair blind spot (co-encode → can't self-remove) | ρ = −0.81 | **gone** by 7–9B (ρ = −0.20; self is best) |

Distinct family bias directions persist at scale, yet by 7–9B a model has enough
edit capacity plus self-matched elicitation to overcome any co-encoding
disadvantage and repair itself — better than out-family designers. **Durable
geometry, non-durable blind spot.** The self-repair blind spot is bounded to the
small-model regime.

Open (by choice, not blockers): 70–72B designers for R2's strict 70B claim; a
size-matched cross-designer T2 to fully isolate co-encoding from designer size;
more seeds/families to tighten the n=4 T2 estimate.
