`resolve_targets(model, ATTN)` was evaluated on all nine panel models. Eight
return `['q_proj','k_proj','v_proj','o_proj']`. **Phi-3.5-mini returns

## 1. ENV — the operating-envelope gate does NOT survive held-out validation

experiments_v8 §ENV expected this section to "clear the `[TODO: τ/γ]` and upgrade
Contribution 4 from conditional to claimed." **On the measured data it does not,
and the upgrade is not earned.**

**Inventory.** 36 (target, axis) cells under the self designer across four
benchmark families (templated 9, CrowS 17, BBQ 8, StereoSet 2) — not the ~18–20
v8 estimated. The widening comes from reading `contrast_gap` out of the corpus
file each run actually consumed rather than from `contrast_gap_frozen.json`,
which covers only `occ_gender`/`crows_socioeconomic` (frozen 2026-07-29, before
the BBQ and StereoSet runs). The 114 frozen observations were cross-checked
against the on-disk corpora: **0 disagreements**.

**Split**, stratified by benchmark family, frozen and hashed before any threshold
was searched: 17 calibration / 16 held-out / 3 excluded
(`results/v8/env_split.json`, sha256 `aded438e…6842cb`). Excluded:
`granite × occ_gender` (granite, granite2, granite8), 100% budget-fail at every α
for every designer — prereg V3 collateral fragility, invisible to a
`(pre_skew, contrast_gap)` gate and therefore poison for it.

**On calibration the gate does exactly what the theory says.** τ = 0.1158 on
`|pre_skew|`, γ = 0.0116 on `contrast_gap`; the joint gate rejects 4 of 17 cells,
three of which are backfires:

| | edit-all | joint gate |
|---|---:|---:|
| utility (abstain = 0) | +0.1462 | **+0.1564** |
| backfire rate (edited) | 0.18 | **0.00** |
| edit rate | 1.00 | 0.76 |

**On held-out it is untestable.** The frozen split placed **zero** held-out cells
below either threshold (held-out min `|pre_skew|` = 0.151 vs τ = 0.1158; min
`contrast_gap` = +0.016 vs γ = +0.0116), so all four policies degenerate to
edit-all:

| policy | mean removal (edited) | backfire | edit rate | utility (abstain = 0) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| edit-all | +0.1900 | 0.06 | 1.00 | +0.1900 [+0.083, +0.317] |
| pre-skew gate | +0.1900 | 0.06 | 1.00 | +0.1900 [+0.083, +0.317] |
| contrast-gap gate | +0.1900 | 0.06 | 1.00 | +0.1900 [+0.083, +0.317] |
| joint gate | +0.1900 | 0.06 | 1.00 | +0.1900 [+0.083, +0.317] |

The low-bias regime the gate exists to detect happens to live entirely in the
calibration half. **The split was not re-drawn** — it is frozen and hashed, and
re-drawing to obtain a discriminating held-out set is the exact failure the
freeze exists to prevent.

**The registered sensitivity does discriminate, and it is negative.** Unit
(target, axis, designer), n = 87 held-out:

| policy | mean removal (edited) | backfire | edit rate | utility |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| edit-all | +0.2632 | 0.11 | 1.00 | +0.2632 [+0.204, +0.323] |
| pre-skew gate | +0.2735 | 0.10 | 0.97 | +0.2641 [+0.205, +0.324] |
| contrast-gap gate | +0.2768 | 0.08 | 0.84 | **+0.2323** [+0.175, +0.292] |
| joint gate | +0.2735 | 0.10 | 0.97 | +0.2641 [+0.205, +0.324] |

With 87 held-out units the pre-skew gate buys **+0.0009 utility** over editing
everything, and the contrast-gap gate *costs* utility (+0.2323 vs +0.2632) to buy
a backfire reduction of 0.03. The gate that looked decisive on 17 calibration
cells — backfire 0.18 → 0.00 on four rejections — does not reproduce.

**Was seed 0 simply unlucky? Checked, and the answer does not rescue the gate.**
Re-running the *split rule* over 200 seeds (the frozen seed-0 split remains the
primary result and no threshold was re-chosen) shows a non-discriminating
held-out half is **rare — 13 of 200 seeds, 6%**. So the degenerate policy table
above is specific to seed 0 rather than a property of the rule. But across the
**187 seeds where the held-out half *can* discriminate**, the joint gate's utility
gain over edit-all is **mean −0.0012, median +0.0002, and exceeds +0.01 in only
6.4% of splits**. The gate is not being hidden by an unlucky draw; it is
genuinely not there.

This is reported because it makes the negative *stronger*, and it is the only
legitimate use of a re-split: it changes no threshold, rescues no claim, and the
frozen split stays primary.

**Honest conclusion.** `contrast_gap` remains a good *predictor* of removal
(+0.507 [+0.220, +0.732], n = 504, unchanged from §10 of the method results);
what fails is turning it plus `pre_skew` into an **abstention policy that beats
editing everything**. That failure now rests on three independent looks — the
frozen held-out split, the registered n=87 designer-level sensitivity, and 187
discriminating re-splits — so it is not an artifact of one draw. Contribution 4
stays **conditional**. The manuscript should
say the envelope is *described and calibrated* but not validated out-of-sample,
and report both tables above. This is the eleventh operationalisation in this
program to fail its own pre-registered test, and it fails the same way the other
ten did: convincing in-sample, absent out-of-sample.

Figure: `results/figures/fig_env_envelope.png` (v8 Fig 6). The left panel shows why
the held-out split cannot discriminate — every held-out cell sits to the right of
τ. The labelled points are the backfires, and three of the four live in

## 2. Reproducibility — bit-exact across a library-stack change

The analysis box was re-provisioned between v7 and v8: `transformers`/`peft` were
absent and were reinstalled at **transformers 4.57.1 / peft 0.20.0 /
torch 2.11.0+cu128**, against v5-era **transformers 5.14.1 / peft 0.19.1**.

DEC's C-ref condition is by construction the existing 99%-sparse edit. On
`gemma|occ_gender|s0|self` it returns **+0.1353**, against the `ablate` panel's
`binary-per_tensor-s0.99` value of **+0.1353** — measured 2026-08-03 on the old
stack. Bit-identity of the *construction* is asserted on every run
(`src/v8_edits.selftest()`: max |diff| = 0.000e+00 vs
`binarize(v,"per_tensor",0.99,seed)`) and by `src/v8_selftest.py` (45/45).

**Scope of the determinism claim, corrected by audit.** The **edit construction**
is bit-identical across the library change; the **evaluation** is not fully
deterministic. Of 48 (target, seed, sparsity) rows shared between `v8spc/small`
and `v6trace/ablate`, **46 are bit-identical and 2 differ** — both
`llama|occ_gender|s2` — with *identical* `ppl_ratio` and `bias_reduction` but
different `dmmlu`, i.e. MMLU item-level nondeterminism (plausibly bf16 reduction
order). Because the collateral gate is a hard threshold, that ±1-item noise flips
`collateral_ok` and swings recorded removal by up to **0.206**. So §8's
"bit-deterministic" should be read as *the pipeline is deterministic up to
MMLU-item noise, which the budget gate amplifies* — see §7.

### Two engineering notes, in the spirit of §8

**`binarize()` held a full extra copy of the contrast vector, and it killed a run.**
The magnitude threshold is computed as
`flat = torch.cat([t.abs().flatten() for t in v.values()]); flat.kthvalue(k)`,
and `flat` stayed in scope for the rest of the function while the edit `out` was
being built. On llama-3.1-8B the attention set is 1.34e9 parameters, so `v` alone
is 5.4 GB fp32 and the live set was `v` + `flat` + `out` ≈ 16 GB. `run_spc.py`
was SIGKILLed (rc=137) at 37/48 rows on the 7–9B tier **running alone** — this was
not a concurrency failure.

Fixed by deleting `flat` immediately after the threshold is taken (and `allv`
likewise in the `scalar` branch). `thresh` is already a Python float by then, so
nothing downstream changes: verified **bit-identical across all three
granularities × sparsity {0, 0.5, 0.99, 0.999}**, and C-ref's identity with the
frozen `binarize` output re-asserted. This is the same class of fix as §8's
in-place `contrast_` (34 GB → 17.6 GB, bit-exact); the peak here drops by the
5.4 GB copy. SPC's 7–9B tier was resumed on the patched code, keeping the 37 rows
that had completed.

**One concurrency lesson, recorded because it cost a run.** `run_sup1_designers.py`
was SIGKILLed (rc=137) while sharing the box with `run_dec_natural.py` — a 7B and
a 3B delta-materialising job together exceeded 31 GB. Nothing was lost (both are
resumable and completed dumps are skipped), but the rule that follows is
"never run two delta-materialising jobs at once", not "check free RAM first":
the check passed at 27 GB available and the peak still collided.

## 3. INS — gate PASSED, arm re-scoped

**Gate:** lm-evaluation-harness runs IFEval + MMLU on our checkpoints within one
working day. **PASS**, in ~40 minutes. The gate was tested against the thing that
could actually fail — not whether the harness installs, but whether it can score
an **in-memory edited model**, since a checkpoint per condition (~15 GB × 5
conditions × 2 targets × 3 seeds) is impossible on a box with 22 GB free.
`HFLM(pretrained=<live model object>)` works; MMLU and IFEval both run; and a
sensitivity check confirms the harness reads the *edited* weights — zeroing
layer-0 `q_proj` moves `inst_level_strict_acc` 0.367 → 0.300.

### INS-A — generation collateral: no instruction-following cost attributable to the edit

The first generation-based collateral this project has measured; the budget has
always been likelihood-only (MMLU + perplexity). Always-on, seed 0, IFEval on the
**first 200 of 541 prompts** — a stated sampling bound, SE ≈ 0.020 on
`inst_level_strict` (~600 atomic instructions).

| target | condition | removal | inst_strict | vs unedited |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| qwen7b_it | unedited | — | 0.6069 | — |
| qwen7b_it | fp | +0.6965 | 0.6289 | +0.022 |
| qwen7b_it | sign_only | +0.6980 | 0.6384 | +0.031 |
| qwen7b_it | sparse99 | +0.6652 | 0.6258 | +0.019 |
| qwen7b_it | random_sign | +0.0037 | 0.6384 | +0.031 |
| llama8b_it | unedited | — | 0.6164 | — |
| llama8b_it | fp | +0.7422 | 0.5755 | −0.041 |

| llama8b_it | sparse99 | +0.3098 | 0.5912 | −0.025 |
| llama8b_it | random_sign | +0.0012 | 0.6069 | −0.009 |

**Read the matched-norm contrast, not the raw delta.** `random_sign` also moves

- **llama8b_it: −0.0220** — about 1.1 SE, within noise.

So instruction following is not measurably damaged by the sign structure on either
target. This is the deployment answer the likelihood-only budget could not give.
Bounds to state with it: 2 targets, 1 axis, seed 0, 200 of 541 prompts.

A side observation consistent with §6: `sparse99` holds up on qwen
(+0.665 vs +0.698 dense) but costs heavily on llama (+0.310 vs +0.735) — the same
target-dependence of the 99%-sparsity claim that SPC quantifies.

### INS-B / BP — the base↔instruct comparison is CONFOUNDED, and none of the registered readings is licensed

Arm B (chat template) failed on all six base cells for the structural reason in
§0b. Arm BP forced the **same plain-text template on both checkpoints** and ran
to completion (48 rows). The result is a negative one about the *experiment*, not
about instruction tuning.

**The format control was necessary.** Comparing `gemma2b_it` across arms:
`pre_skew` is **identical** (+0.8598) — the bias probe uses `cont_loglik` on raw
prefixes and never touches `chat_prompt` — but **MMLU falls 0.625 → 0.450**, a
−0.175 prompt-format effect, because `mmlu_predict` does go through it. Under the
naive fix (plain text for base, chat template for instruct) that −0.175 would have
been silently charged to instruction tuning.

**Gates under the common format:**

| target | axis | pre_skew | MMLU | ppl |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| gemma2b_base | occ_gender | +0.7079 | **0.190** | 15.64 |
| gemma2b_it | occ_gender | +0.8598 | 0.450 | 22.82 |
| gemma2b_base | bbq_Age | +0.4359 | **0.190** | 15.64 |
| gemma2b_it | bbq_Age | +0.2896 | 0.450 | 22.82 |

**Removal** (mean over 3 seeds; instruct\|bbq_Age has 6 budget-fail rows → 0):

| axis | condition | base | instruct |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| occ_gender | sign_only | +0.6047 | +0.5718 |
| occ_gender | random_sign | +0.0004 | +0.0030 |
| bbq_Age | sign_only | +0.3853 | +0.0585 |
| bbq_Age | random_sign | +0.0019 | +0.0006 |

Read naively this says *instruction tuning moves bbq_Age out of the envelope* —
registered reading (ii). **That reading is not licensed, because the collateral
budget is not comparable across the two checkpoints.**

**Base MMLU is 0.190, below 4-way chance (0.250).** The base model cannot do the
MCQ task in this format at all, so its MMLU is noise around a floor and the
`ΔMMLU ≥ −0.02` criterion can barely bind. The budget is effectively
**two-sided for the instruct arm and ppl-only for the base arm**. The binding
criterion among out-of-budget α confirms it directly:

| target | ppl-bound | MMLU-bound | both |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| gemma2b_base | 18 | **3** | 0 |
| gemma2b_it | 7 | **26** | 17 |

So the base arm passes the budget more often because it faces a weaker constraint,
not necessarily because the edit works better on it. The bbq_Age gap
(+0.385 vs +0.059) is exactly where instruct failures are MMLU-driven — the one
criterion the base model is structurally incapable of failing.

**What INS-B does license**, and it is worth stating: on occ_gender the base model
is editable at all, +0.6047 against its own matched-norm null of +0.0004
(`sign_only − random_sign` = **+0.6044**). **The sparse-sign phenomenon is not an
artifact of instruction tuning** — it works on a pre-RLHF checkpoint. That is a
real finding and it is the first base-model measurement in this program's method
line.

**What it does not license:** any claim about the *relative* size of the effect
across base and instruct, and therefore none of the three registered claim-ladder
rungs. Reporting reading (ii) here would have been wrong. The comparison needs a
collateral metric the base model can actually perform — a likelihood task rather
than a chat-formatted MCQ — and that is a new evaluation stack, not a re-run. This
is registered as the honest outcome and the ladder is left unclimbed.

**The claim.** Δ_selection is +0.2537 (**79% of R(C-ref)** *at the frozen 4-α
grid* — see the censoring caveat below), and its CI excludes 0. Behavioural edits
do **not** behave like the task-performance deltas DARE studies: a random 1% of
the sign field recovers only about a fifth of what the magnitude-selected 1%
recovers. The effect concentrates in a **magnitude-identifiable sparse signed
substructure**.

**Robust to the §7 floating-point fix.** Re-scored with an ε tolerance on the
collateral gate, Δ_selection is **+0.2835 [+0.1692, +0.4072]**, still excluding 0
and still unanimous 10/10 — the correction *strengthens* the result, because
C-ref recovers more boundary-rejected α than C-a does (R(C-ref) +0.322 → +0.353
vs C-a +0.068 → +0.070).

**Two conditioning facts to state in the paper.** (i) The 10 cells were
pre-registered as *in-envelope*, i.e. selected because C-ref already showed
positive removal — which conditions the "79%" denominator and means the ratio is
not an unconditional effect size. (ii) The magnitude is bounded by the α grid
(below); the sign is not.

**It is unanimous, not driven by a subset.** Δ_selection > 0 in **10 of 10 cells**,
spanning 48%–103% of each cell's R(C-ref), across four benchmark families and both
size tiers:

**The nuance that keeps this honest, and it favours DARE.** C-a is not dead:
**C-a − C-rand = +0.0670, CI excludes 0.** The true sign field evaluated at
*random* coordinates does carry real signal — about 21% of what it carries at
magnitude-selected coordinates. So the field is not purely local to the top 1%;
it is *concentrated* there. The paper should say concentrated, not exclusive.

**Magnitude ranking is informative in both directions.** The registered
Δ_ranking ordering comes out C-ref (+0.322) ≫ C-a (+0.068) > C-bottom (+0.001),
with C-a − C-bottom also excluding 0. Bottom-magnitude coordinates carrying the
true signs are indistinguishable from the complete null. This is what
distinguishes "magnitude ranking finds the structure" from "any high-magnitude
set works" — the discrimination runs the whole length of the ranking.

**The nulls behave.** C-rand averages **+0.0014** against the pre-registered
expectation of ≈+0.008, and is ≤ +0.0041 in every cell.

### α-grid censoring: a real asymmetry that bounds the magnitude, not the sign

The 4-α grid stops at 16, so a condition whose optimum lies beyond it is censored.
**C-a is censored much more often than C-ref** — α=16 is the in-budget argmax in
**86.7%** of C-a cells against **46.7%** for C-ref — which on its face would
inflate Δ_selection. Three measurements say it does not:

| condition | α=2 | α=4 | α=8 | α=16 | slope 8→16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| C-ref | +0.064 | +0.145 | +0.244 | +0.291 | **+0.091** |
| C-a | +0.004 | +0.010 | +0.022 | +0.064 | **+0.041** |
| C-rand | −0.000 | −0.000 | +0.000 | +0.000 | ~0 |

1. **The two arms are censored by different constraints, and this is a real
   asymmetry, not a defence.** C-ref is **collateral-limited**: it has an
   in-budget α=16 in only 20 of 30 runs, and where it survives it is consuming
   real budget (ppl up to 1.358). C-a is **purely grid-limited**: its argmax is at
   α=16 in 26 of 30 runs with `ppl_ratio` confined to 0.991–1.012 and
   |ΔMMLU| ≤ 0.02 — essentially no collateral consumed. So the comparison is *not*
   "each condition evaluated at its own collateral frontier"; C-ref is at its
   frontier and C-a is at the edge of the grid. **This is a genuine limitation of
   the 4-α design and should be stated in the paper**, not argued away.
2. **Bound, stated honestly.** Grant C-a one further doubling at its own 8→16 rate
   and grant C-ref nothing: Δ_selection falls +0.2537 → **+0.2131**, still outside
   the CI's +0.1475 floor. But the audit's more aggressive counterfactual —
   continuing *all* 26 grid-capped C-a runs along their observed curves — gives
   **+0.0957 [−0.0095, +0.2021], covering 0**. Restricting continuation to the 12
   runs whose α-curve is verifiably near-linear gives +0.2158 with unanimity
   falling to 9/10.
3. **Reading.** Outcome B's *sign* is robust; its *magnitude* is an upper bound at
   the frozen grid. The "79% of R(C-ref)" figure should be reported as
   grid-conditional, not as a scale-free effect size. The grid was not widened
   because that would break numeric comparability with RESULTS_METHOD §5 and the
   equal-selection-space rule — which is a reason to caveat the number, not a
   reason to treat it as unconditional.

**Two reporting caveats, both structural.**

1. **Per-condition means are over different cell sets.** C-tensorshuf and every
   LOPO condition are n = 7, because the architecture guard marks them
   not-applicable on phi's fused-`qkv_proj` cells (§0a). So the raw means are not
   directly comparable — e.g. `C-ref-no_q_proj` reads +0.4111 against C-ref's
   +0.3220, but that is n=7 against n=10; the *paired* estimand on the shared
   cells is −0.0105 and covers 0. Only the paired estimands are valid, and the
   figure labels the n=7 bars.
2. **The scale convention did not manufacture the result — it worked against it.**
   The registered `natural`-scale arm (2 cells × 3 seeds) is complete:

   | cell | convention | C-ref | C-a | Δ_selection | ‖C-a‖/‖C-ref‖ |
   |---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | gemma\|occ_gender | ref_tensor | +0.5123 | +0.0823 | +0.4300 | **0.872** |
   | gemma\|occ_gender | **natural** | +0.5123 | +0.0092 | **+0.5031** | 0.141 |
   | qwen\|bbq_Age | ref_tensor | +0.2531 | +0.1327 | +0.1204 | **0.940** |
   | qwen\|bbq_Age | **natural** | +0.2531 | +0.0258 | **+0.2273** | 0.165 |

   C-ref is numerically identical under both conventions, as it must be by
   construction — a useful internal check. Under `natural`, C-a receives only
   **14–17%** of C-ref's edit norm and its removal collapses; Δ_selection grows in
   **both** cells. No direction reversal, so the registered "headline caveat"
   branch does not fire. **`ref_tensor` is the conservative convention** — it gives

   **Correction (audit, 2026-08-29):** an earlier version of this table printed

   synthetic tensors, where the two supports distribute alike.

## 7. A floating-point bug in the collateral gate — found by audit, affects every panel

`colab_t2t4.collateral_ok` (line 673) tests

```python
(pre["mmlu_acc"] - post["mmlu_acc"]) <= dmmlu        # dmmlu = 0.02
```

MMLU is scored on **200 items**, so accuracy moves in steps of 0.005 and a
**4-item drop is exactly the 0.02 budget**. In IEEE-754 it is not:
`0.545 - 0.525 == 0.020000000000000018 > 0.02`. Configurations that sit precisely
**on** the budget are therefore rejected.

This is not a v8 artifact. Sweeping every persisted α trace in the repository:

| panel | boundary-rejected probes | of total | rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| v6trace/ablate | 36 | 1344 | 2.7% |
| v8dec | 28 | 1140 | 2.5% |
| v6trace_3b/small | 19 | 516 | 3.7% |
| v6trace/mvb | 16 | 792 | 2.0% |
| v6trace/ax_null_sign_shuffle | 15 | 192 | 7.8% |
| v6trace/fxnull_sign_shuffle | 11 | 72 | 15.3% |
| … 19 further panels | | | |
| **total** | **238** | | **25 panels** |

These are probes rejected *only* by the boundary — perplexity was inside budget
and ΔMMLU was within 1e-9 of the limit.

**Impact, measured by re-scoring from the α traces** (no re-running needed — this
is exactly what v6's alpha-trace persistence was added for):

| result | as-run | ε-corrected | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| DEC Δ_selection | +0.2537 [+0.1475, +0.3692] | **+0.2835 [+0.1692, +0.4072]** | unchanged, 10/10 unanimous |
| SPC ≤3.8B, 99% vs dense | −0.1286 [−0.2077, −0.0308] | **−0.0754 [−0.1665, +0.0158]** | **flips to covering 0** |

DEC's headline is *strengthened* (C-ref recovers more of the boundary than C-a:
R(C-ref) +0.322 → +0.353 against C-a's +0.068 → +0.070). SPC's ≤3.8B claim does
**not** survive — see §6, which has been retracted accordingly.

**Two compounding problems, both stated rather than fixed here.** The MMLU
granularity (0.005 on 200 items) is coarse relative to a 0.02 budget, so the gate
turns a 4-item difference into a pass/fail. And the evaluation is **not fully
deterministic**: two `llama|occ_gender|s2` rows shared between `v8spc/small` and
`v6trace/ablate` have bit-identical `ppl_ratio` and `bias_reduction` but different
`dmmlu`, flipping `collateral_ok` and swinging recorded removal by 0.206.

**Recommended fix, not applied:** compare with an explicit tolerance
(`<= dmmlu + 1e-9`) or in exact rational arithmetic, and raise the MMLU item count
so the budget is not a small multiple of the metric's granularity. Applying it
would change numbers across the whole program, so it is a decision for the author,
not a silent edit — but the current `RESULTS_METHOD` figures are all measured with
the boundary bug present.
